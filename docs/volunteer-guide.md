# Volunteer guide

Use the [public application form](https://github.com/Pick-Your-AI/pick-your-ai-volunteers/issues/new?template=application.yml). Select any combination of Team, Repository, Project and Opportunity, list alternatives, or choose “Help me find where I am most useful.” These are distinct entities: joining a team is not required to express interest in a repository. You can browse all opportunities without recommendations.

Describe your desired role, relevant skills, optional public work, approximate availability and mentoring needs. Do not include legal names, contact details, health information, sensitive background, passwords or customer material. A GitHub account is needed to submit. Applications and their status comments are visible to everyone, including search engines. No confidential intake or private status tracking is available. Private repositories do not provide per-applicant isolation and are not used for intake.

A reviewer first confirms a bounded task, authority and capacity. They may request information, accept, decline with a reason, propose a referral, or request escalation. Unknown capacity means waiting, not rejection. There is no promised response deadline while reviewer coverage is unconfirmed. Acceptance is separate from onboarding, invitations and active placement.

Referrals require your agreement each time. Use the referral form to request a change, linking your application. Your original interest remains visible. Only consent on the original application can approve a proposed placement change. You may decline a referral and continue at the original route. A form or comment never grants membership or permissions.

After acceptance, agree a small first task, success criteria, time and communication expectations, review contact, documentation and support needs. The receiving owner confirms onboarding capacity. Read the target contribution guide and conduct policy; use forks where possible. Check in at an agreed cadence and adjust workload freely. Do not assume any named mentor is available.

To withdraw, comment on your application:

```text
/volunteer {"action":"withdraw","reason":"I am withdrawing my interest."}
```

This remains available even while decision authority is inactive. Withdrawing ends the application workflow; it does not erase GitHub history or revoke access. You can also ask to leave in ordinary language, for operator follow-up. Removing previously granted access requires a separate authorized administrator action. Do not post private departure reasons. Agree handoff of open work without making continued volunteering a condition of leaving. Corrections and content removal requests go through the existing GitHub/organization mechanisms; permanent erasure from clones, caches or Git history cannot be promised.
